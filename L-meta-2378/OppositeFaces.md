# A restriction on a possible 103-OR circuit

Let the 21 point inputs be the first block and the 21 line inputs be the second. Suppose that, after fixing every point input to false, wires of the circuit compute all 21 five-line neighborhood disjunctions. Then any fan-in-two monotone circuit computing the projective-plane edge function uses at least 104 OR gates. The neighborhood wires can have arbitrary dependencies on the point inputs elsewhere.

The line-only restriction needs at least 84 OR gates whose outputs vary on that face. To see this, simplify constant wires and take the last AND gate in a circuit that produces the 21 required linear outputs. Let P be the set of singleton line inputs on which the AND outputs true. Only ORs can occur between this last AND and an affected output. When P has at most one member, replace the AND by zero or by that input. This weakens every output while preserving the value on every singleton, which suffices for these disjunctions. When P has two or more members, at most one target output depends on the AND, since two distinct neighborhoods share only one line. On the maximal negative input for that output, at least one operand of the AND is false. Replacing the AND by that operand increases the output while leaving it false on that maximal negative input. This preserves all its values by monotonicity. Constant gates can be simplified first. Iterating gives an OR-only circuit with no increase in OR cost. Each five-input sum needs four gates depending on at least two distinct lines, and each such gate feeds at most one of the 21 sums. This is the disjoint-sums argument of Mehlhorn specialized to this instance. [Mehlhorn, proof of Lemma 1](https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=e3379453f082008ccb0af8ad2343089fcc298c6a&repid=rep1&type=pdf)

On the second face, fix every line input to true. The required scalar output is now the OR of 21 point inputs, requiring another 20 varying OR gates. The preceding last-AND elimination also proves this one-output case. The two sets of varying gates are disjoint. At their shared input, with the points false and lines true, a signal varying on the first face is true because this input is its maximum. A signal varying on the second face is false because the same input is its minimum. Lean checked this disjointness and counting step for the project's actual monotone circuit wires. [OppositeFaces.lean](OppositeFaces.lean)

Consequently a successful circuit must avoid producing the entire set of neighborhood disjunctions even under the first restriction. Interchanging point and line roles gives the dual statement. The argument leaves open circuits that only produce pieces of the neighborhoods.

A different restriction gives an unconditional baseline of 33 OR gates. Set points 8, 10, 16, 19 and lines 1, 11, 14, 20 to true, indexing each shore from zero as in the desk statement. This is a maximal independent set of eight vertices. Every other variable creates an incidence when it is switched on. The restricted function is therefore the OR of 34 unrestricted inputs. Lean proves the resulting lower bound for the exact gate-index count in the desk statement. Its backward trace preserves a false output by keeping one input false at a false AND gate and at most two false at a false OR gate. An input whose individual flip would turn on the output must occur in this trace. [MaximalCorner.lean](MaximalCorner.lean)
