# Four cyclically permuted rows in the 5 by 5 permanent

**Proposition.** Let J be the all-ones matrix of size five. If a complex linear space L is invariant under a cyclic permutation of four rows fixing the fifth and per(J+L)=120 identically, then dim L ≤ 7. The seven-dimensional example in the earlier research packet has this symmetry, so this bound is sharp in the specified class. The general eight-dimensional question remains open here. This is a written proof.

## 1. Character components

Call the fixed row the top row. The four lower rows have root labels r=1,i,-1,-i. By Fourier projections L splits into its fixed part F and three parts indexed by k=1,2,3. A vector in a nonfixed part has top row zero and lower entries r^k v_j for a column vector v in a space V_k inside C^5. Write U=V_1, H=V_2, V=V_3. Write e_d for elementary symmetric polynomials.

All identities used below follow by expansion over matchings. For an ordered list of modes (k1,...,kd), the sum of row weights over distinct choices of lower rows is A=sum product r_a^k_a over injections of the d slots into the four roots. It is independent of the columns chosen, and conjugation interchanges modes 1 and 3. The row weights used below are A1111=A3333=-24, A22=-4, A2222=24, A13=-4, A1133=8, A112=A332=8. Overall completion factors, factorials from indistinguishable slots and root weight coefficients are all nonzero. Thus for one vector from each of U,H,V, written u,h,v:

- e4 vanishes on all three spaces, and e2 vanishes on H.
- B(u,v)=(sum u)(sum v)−sum_j u_jv_j=0.
- The sum of the terms u_a u_b v_c v_d over unordered disjoint pairs {a,b},{c,d} is zero. This identity may be polarized independently in u,v.

A space W of dimension at least two with e4|W=0 is of one of two types. With at most three active coordinates, it is supported on a coordinate triple. With exactly four active coordinates the product of the four is a nonzero polynomial and contradicts e4=0. If all five are active, divide e4 by their product to obtain the rational identity sum 1/x_j=0 on W, where each x_j is a nonzero linear functional. For each proportionality class of functionals, the inverse coefficients in that class must sum to zero. To see this multiply by the product of one representative per class and restrict to that class's kernel at a point off the other kernels. Thus each class has size at least two. Since coordinate functionals span W*, dim W ≤ 2, and at dimension two there are two classes of sizes two and three. The space is spanned by a pair-supported vector (a,-a) and a disjoint triple vector b with all entries nonzero and e2(b)=0.

In particular dim H ≤ 1. Indeed on a coordinate triple e2 is nondegenerate and has isotropic dimension at most one. A dense two-plane has a pair direction with e2=-a² ≠ 0. Also dim U, dim V ≤ 3, with equality implying a full coordinate triple.

## 2. The two odd modes together have dimension at most three

If U is the full coordinate triple S, then v_j=t=sum v for j in S by B=0. Write p,q for the other two entries, so p+q=−2t. The quartic e4(v)=0 yields t²(3pq−2t²)=0. Polarizing the 1133 identity with two different coordinate units of S gives e2(t,p,q)=0, or pq=2t². Hence t=0 and p=q=0. The same holds with U and V interchanged.

It remains to exclude dim U=dim V=2. If U is dense, use the two vectors (a,-a) on its pair and b on its complementary triple from section 1. Orthogonality to the pair forces v_1=v_2, labeling that pair 1,2. The 1133 identity with the pair vector used twice gives e2 of the remaining triple of v equal to zero. Its projected dimension is at most one by nondegeneracy. Therefore V contains w=(1,1,0,0,0), and B(w,b)=0 gives sum b=0. Subtract multiples of w to see that V is spanned by w and a vector h supported on the triple. For the whole two-plane e4(sw+th)=s²t²e2(h)+2st³e3(h)=0. Thus e2(h)=e3(h)=0, which implies h is supported at a single coordinate j. Then B(b,h)=0 gives b_j=sum b=0, contradicting that b is nonzero at every triple coordinate. This also handles a dense V by symmetry.

If the support of U has size two, U contains two coordinate units e_a,e_b. Thus B gives v_a=v_b=sum v. Polarizing 1133 on e_a,e_b gives e2 on the complementary three v entries equal to zero, so their projected space has dimension at most one. The projection is injective on V since a vector supported on a,b with v_a=v_b=sum v must vanish. This excludes dim V=2, also after interchanging U and V.

Finally let both spaces be supported on triples S,T. If the triples intersect in two or three cells, the 3 by 3 pairing B between their coordinate triples has matrix all ones except for zeros at the shared diagonal positions. Its determinant is nonzero, so it cannot pair two two-dimensional spaces to zero. Triples in five indices intersect at least once. If the intersection has size one, the matrix has rank two. Its left and right radicals are the differences of the two exclusive coordinates in each triple. For two orthogonal two-planes to occur, both must contain their respective radical: otherwise one projects onto the two-dimensional quotient, forcing the other's two-plane into its one-dimensional radical. Insert both difference vectors twice into the 1133 identity. Their disjoint pair supports leave a single nonzero product, a contradiction. We conclude dim U+dim V ≤ 3. Together the three nonfixed components have dimension at most four.

## 3. A fixed component of dimension four

On F the four lower perturbation rows coincide. Call the common row b and the top perturbation a. Two nonproportional nonzero coordinate functionals b_j,b_k could be prescribed simultaneously to −1, leaving four rows supported on at most three columns in J+T and forcing permanent zero. Equal nonzero coordinate functionals would have the same contradiction. Hence the possible b form at most a line, and for a generator of that line its nonzero coefficients are pairwise distinct.

If the bottom row never varies then F is a zero-sum subspace of the top row and has dimension at most four. If it does vary, choose a generator b. Top-only directions a in the kernel satisfy identically sum_j a_j product_{k≠j}(1+z b_k)=0 by linearity in that top direction. Divide by the product and use partial fractions; it follows that the sum of a_j over each group of equal b_j is zero (including the group with zero coefficient, if present). If m is the number of distinct b entries, this gives dim F ≤ 1+5−m. In particular dim F ≤4, and equality can occur only if b has exactly one nonzero entry, at a column j. Rescale it to 1. Write O for the four other columns. The kernel directions then give all top-only vectors with a_j=0 and sum_O a=0. The elementary expansion in the single varying lower column gives per(J+zT)=120+24z(sum a+4)+24z²sum_O a. Thus a_j=−4 and sum_O a=0. Subtracting the top kernel, F contains the direction D with lower row e_j, top row −4e_j.

An eight-dimensional example would require dim F=4, dim H=1, and dim U+dim V=3. We finish by excluding these simultaneously.

## 4. One even mode forces the odd modes onto few columns

For nonfixed lower-row directions let C_k denote the lower four-row permanent after deleting column k, including the base ones. If F consists of all centered top directions then C_k are all equal. Their sum is 120, so their nonconstant coefficients all vanish. The pure h² coefficient in C_k gives e2(h excluding k)=0 for each k. In general, for a vector x of length N≥3, e2(x excluding k)=0 for every k implies support of x has size at most one: summing gives (N−2)e2(x)=0, and subtracting gives x_k(sum x−x_k)=0 for all k. If sum x is zero every entry vanishes, otherwise exactly one nonzero entry can sum to that value. Hence each h is supported in a single column.

For the other possibility in section 3, use D. Adding tD to a pure mode-2 perturbation s h gives C_k(s,t) equal for k in O by the top kernel. The coefficient of t s² in these C_k is a common nonzero factor times e2(h on O excluding k): two mode-2 choices use distinct rows and two columns in O excluding k, and D's lower entry uses column j in either remaining row. The coefficient of t s² in the entire 5 by 5 permanent also gives e2(h on O)=0. In detail, the top entry −4t times C_j contributes −4 times the two-matching coefficient on lower rows and O. Using a lower t instead gives 4−2=2 times the same coefficient, because a matching of size two leaves two lower rows available and the same completion factorial once the t-entry is prescribed. Their sum is −2 times that coefficient, nonzero unless e2(h on O)=0. Therefore all four e2(h on O excluding k) are equal with sum 2e2(h on O)=0. The elementary observation gives at most one active entry in O. The pure identity e2(h)=0 forces h to have at most one active entry anywhere.

Fix a nonzero h, supported in column m. In either possibility for F, the degree-three coefficient in C_k using h and two copies of u, k≠m, is a common nonzero factor (from A112) times e2(u excluding k,m). The coefficient in C_m is zero. If F is the top-only case, all coefficients already vanish. Otherwise they are equal on O and their sum over all five columns vanishes, because one may set all fixed parameters to zero. When m lies in O their common value vanishes immediately; when m=j their sum is four times that common value. Thus they all vanish. The elementary observation on the four entries outside m shows that each u has at most one nonzero coordinate outside m. Since U is linear over C, all of U must be supported in {m,a} for a single a (a vector space cannot be covered by finitely many proper subspaces). Replacing A112 with A332 proves that V is supported on {m,b} for a single b.

For dim U+dim V=3, one of them must consist of all vectors on {m,a} and the other has a nonzero w supported on {m,b}. Orthogonality to both coordinate units gives w_m=w_a=sum w. If a=b the sum equals twice w_m, forcing w=0. If a≠b, w_a=0, and w_m=sum w=0 again forces w=0. This final contradiction proves the proposition.

## Equality example

Take all zero-sum directions in the top row (four free parameters), together with the pure first Fourier mode on any three columns (three more). The lower-row perturbations in the latter family have entries r v_j. Giving the whole lower perturbation a scalar z produces a polynomial invariant under z ↦ iz, because this just permutes the four lower rows. It has degree at most three because only three columns can supply a lower perturbation. It is therefore constant in z, reducing the permanent to 24 times the sum of the top entries, which is 120. This is the earlier seven-dimensional example.

## Computation boundary

The argument uses finite coefficient expansions, elementary exact linear algebra, and polynomial identities. No numerical search or assumption that the conjecture is false is used. It does not certify the project's unrestricted Lean statement.

Transposing matrices preserves the permanent, fixes J, and exchanges row permutations with column permutations. The same sharp bound therefore holds for invariance under any single four-cycle of columns. The restriction is on the whole candidate subspace. Individual matrices in an admissible candidate may have additional symmetries.
